About this free internet speed test

SparkNoa is a free internet speed test that measures how fast your connection is right now. In one run it reports your download speed, upload speed, ping (latency), jitter and a rough estimate of latency under load. It works on Wi-Fi, Ethernet, fiber, cable, DSL, 4G and 5G, in any modern browser on a phone, tablet or computer.

Speeds come from moving real data between your browser and Cloudflare Worker endpoints, so the result reflects your whole path: your device, Wi-Fi, router, internet provider and the route to the test server. That makes it a good way to check whether the speed you pay for matches what you actually get.

How to run an accurate speed test

  1. Pause large downloads, cloud backups, game updates and video streams on every device in your home.
  2. For a Wi-Fi check, stand near the router. Then, if you can, repeat the test over an Ethernet cable and compare.
  3. Press GO and wait about 20 seconds. Do not switch tabs while it runs.
  4. Run it two or three times at different times of day. One result is a snapshot, not a guarantee.

What do the speed test results mean?

Download speed

How fast data reaches you, measured in megabits per second (Mbps). It matters for streaming, browsing, downloads and game updates.

Upload speed

How fast you can send data out. It matters for video calls, live streaming, cloud backups and sending large files.

Ping (latency)

The round-trip time to the test server in milliseconds. Lower is better and is especially important for gaming and calls. This test measures HTTP latency, which is slightly higher than a raw network ping.

Jitter

How much latency varies between measurements. High jitter makes voice and video choppy even when average speed looks fine.

Failed probes

The share of latency requests that did not complete. This is not true IP packet loss, but anything above 0% can point to an unstable connection.

Latency under load (bufferbloat)

Compares latency while you download and upload with your idle latency. A big jump means other activity on your network can slow games and calls. The A–F grade here is a rough estimate.

What is a good internet speed?

The speed you need depends on what you do and how many people share the connection. These are rough rules of thumb, not guarantees. Services vary, so add up everything that runs at the same time and leave some headroom.

How to fix a slow internet connection

  • Restart your router and modem. Unplug for about 30 seconds, then plug back in and test again.
  • Move closer or use Ethernet. If a cable is much faster than Wi-Fi, your wireless signal is the bottleneck.
  • Switch Wi-Fi bands. 5 GHz is usually faster at short range, and 2.4 GHz reaches farther through walls.
  • Stop background traffic. Cloud sync, updates and streams on other devices all share your bandwidth.
  • Check for bufferbloat. If latency under load is poor, enabling smart queue management (SQM) or QoS on your router can help.
  • Contact your provider. If a wired test is far below your plan at several times of day, share your results with them.

Internet speed test FAQ

How do I test my internet speed?

Close other downloads and streams, then press the GO button at the top of this page. The test measures latency first, then download speed, then upload speed. It takes roughly 20 seconds and needs no app, account or sign-up.

Is this internet speed test accurate?

It gives a useful estimate, not a laboratory measurement. The test runs in your browser and moves data through Cloudflare Worker endpoints, so the result includes your device, Wi-Fi, router, routing and server load. Run it two or three times at different moments and compare the results instead of trusting a single number.

What is a good download speed?

It depends on what you do. As a rough rule, 5–10 Mbps covers an HD stream, 15–25 Mbps covers a 4K stream, and video calls usually work well with a few Mbps in each direction. If several people share the connection, add their needs together and leave some headroom.

What is a good ping or latency?

Latency is the round-trip time in milliseconds (ms). As a rule of thumb, under 20 ms is excellent, 20–50 ms is good for gaming and calls, 50–100 ms is usable, and above 100 ms is often noticeable in fast-paced games and live calls. This test measures HTTP latency to its test server, which is usually a little higher than a raw network ping.

What is jitter?

Jitter is how much your latency changes from one measurement to the next. A connection can have a low average ping but high jitter, which causes choppy voice calls and stuttering in online games. This test reports the average difference between consecutive latency samples.

What are failed probes? Is that packet loss?

No. Failed probes are the percentage of the test's HTTP latency requests that did not complete. True packet loss is measured at the IP level with tools such as ping or traceroute, which a web page cannot run. A value above 0% still hints at an unstable connection.

What is latency under load (bufferbloat)?

When your connection is busy, delays can grow sharply. This is often called bufferbloat. The test compares latency while it is downloading and uploading with your idle latency and shows a rough A–F estimate. It is an indicator, not a formal bufferbloat benchmark.

Why is my Wi-Fi speed slower than the speed I pay for?

Common reasons are distance from the router, walls and interference, congested 2.4 GHz channels, an old router, other devices using bandwidth, or a plan speed that is a best-case figure. Test next to the router, then test over an Ethernet cable if you can. If the cable is much faster, the bottleneck is your Wi-Fi.

How much data does a speed test use?

A speed test transfers real data. The faster your connection, the more it uses: a fast connection can move hundreds of megabytes in a single test. Avoid running it repeatedly on a metered or limited mobile data plan.

What is the difference between Mbps and MB/s?

Mbps means megabits per second and MB/s means megabytes per second. One byte is eight bits, so divide the Mbps figure by 8 to get MB/s. A 100 Mbps connection tops out at about 12.5 MB/s, which is the number most file download windows show.
