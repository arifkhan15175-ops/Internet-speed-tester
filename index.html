About this free internet speed test

SparkNoa is a free internet speed test that measures how fast your connection is right now. In one run it reports your download speed, upload speed, ping and latency under load so you can judge whether your connection is healthy.

Speeds come from moving real data between your browser and Cloudflare Worker endpoints, so the result reflects your whole path: your device, Wi‑Fi, router, internet provider and the route to the test server.

How to run an accurate speed test

  1. Pause large downloads, cloud backups, game updates and video streams on every device in your home.
  2. For a Wi‑Fi check, stand near the router. Then, if you can, repeat the test over an Ethernet cable and compare.
  3. Press GO and wait about 20 seconds. Do not switch tabs while it runs.
  4. Run it two or three times at different times of day. One result is a snapshot, not a guarantee.

What do the speed test results mean?

Download speed

How fast data reaches you, measured in megabits per second (Mbps). It matters for streaming, browsing, downloads and game updates.

Upload speed

How fast you can send data out. It matters for video calls, live streaming, cloud backups and sending large files.

Ping (latency)

The round-trip time to the test server in milliseconds. Lower is better and is especially important for gaming and calls.

Jitter

How much latency varies between measurements. High jitter makes voice and video choppy even when average speed looks fine.

Failed probes

The share of latency requests that did not complete. This is not true IP packet loss, but anything above 0% can point to an unstable connection.

Latency under load (bufferbloat)

Compares latency while you download and upload with your idle latency. A big jump can make games and calls feel laggy even when the download speed is solid.

What is a good internet speed?

The speed you need depends on what you do and how many people share the connection. These are rough rules of thumb, not guarantees. Services vary, so add up everything that runs at the same time.

Approximate internet speed needed for common activities
ActivityRough speed needed
HD streaming5–10 Mbps
4K streaming25–50 Mbps
Video calls3–10 Mbps each direction
Gaming & callsPing under ~50 ms with low jitter
Large downloads100+ Mbps helps a lot

For gaming and calls, a ping under about 50 ms with low jitter feels smooth. Between 50 and 100 ms is usable, and above 100 ms is often noticeable in fast-paced games.

How to fix a slow internet connection

  • Restart your router and modem. Unplug for about 30 seconds, then plug back in and test again.
  • Move closer or use Ethernet. If a cable is much faster than Wi‑Fi, your wireless signal is the bottleneck.
  • Switch Wi‑Fi bands. 5 GHz is usually faster at short range, and 2.4 GHz reaches farther through walls.
  • Stop background traffic. Cloud sync, updates and streams on other devices all share your bandwidth.
  • Check for bufferbloat. If latency under load is poor, enabling smart queue management (SQM) or QoS on your router can help.
  • Contact your provider. If a wired test is far below your plan at several times of day, share your results with them.

Internet speed test FAQ

How do I test my internet speed?

Close other downloads and streams, then press the GO button at the top of this page. The test measures latency first, then download speed, then upload speed. It takes roughly 20 seconds.

Is this internet speed test accurate?

It gives a useful estimate, not a laboratory measurement. The test runs in your browser and moves data through Cloudflare Worker endpoints, so the result includes your device, Wi‑Fi, router, and internet path.

What is a good download speed?

It depends on what you do. As a rough rule, 5–10 Mbps covers an HD stream, 15–25 Mbps covers a 4K stream, and video calls usually work well with a few Mbps in each direction.

What is a good ping or latency?

Latency is the round-trip time in milliseconds (ms). As a rule of thumb, under 20 ms is excellent, 20–50 ms is good for gaming and calls, 50–100 ms is usable, and above 100 ms is often noticeable.

What is jitter?

Jitter is how much your latency changes from one measurement to the next. A connection can have a low average ping but high jitter, which causes choppy voice calls and stuttery video.

What are failed probes? Is that packet loss?

No. Failed probes are the percentage of the test's HTTP latency requests that did not complete. True packet loss is measured at the IP level with tools such as ping or traceroute.

What is latency under load (bufferbloat)?

When your connection is busy, delays can grow sharply. This is often called bufferbloat. The test compares latency while it is downloading and uploading with your idle latency.

Why is my Wi‑Fi speed slower than the speed I pay for?

Common reasons are distance from the router, walls and interference, congested 2.4 GHz channels, an old router, other devices using bandwidth, or a plan speed that is a best-case estimate.

How much data does a speed test use?

A speed test transfers real data. The faster your connection, the more it uses: a fast connection can move hundreds of megabytes in a single test. Avoid running it repeatedly if you are on a small cellular plan.

What is the difference between Mbps and MB/s?

Mbps means megabits per second and MB/s means megabytes per second. One byte is eight bits, so divide the Mbps figure by 8 to get MB/s. A 100 Mbps connection tops out at about 12.5 MB/s.

Is this speed test free? Do I need to install anything?

It is free, needs no account, and runs in any modern browser on phones, tablets and computers. Your test history stays in your own browser. You can also install it like an app if you want.
